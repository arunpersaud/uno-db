Ratings

A skill rating like the ones for chess or online games (openskill, similar to TrueSkill). All games are replayed in order; winning against strong players raises the rating more than winning against weak ones, and a win at a big table counts more than at a small one. The rating shown is a careful estimate (μ − 3σ): it only gets high with many good games, not with a lucky streak.

How much is skill?

Before each game the model gives every player a chance to win. If the ratings mean something, the favorites should win more often than chance (1 in N at a table of N). Counted over all games after the first 200:

The favorites win somewhat more often than chance, but not by much: a single game is mostly luck. Over hundreds of games, the small differences between the players add up and become clear (see points vs. table). Both ways of ranking predict about equally well, and so does the points average: openskill only uses the order of the points, not how far apart they are. ("Better than guessing" compares the log loss of the predictions with guessing 1 in N; 0% would be no better than chance.)
